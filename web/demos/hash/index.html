Where Keys Live

A hash table is the data structure every language reaches for first — and its whole mystery is one sentence: the hash decides where a key lives, so finding it later needs no search. Eight buckets, a hash you can compute in your head, one collision, and two lookups — every figure below is a declarative scene compiling in your browser.

01

Where keys live

The rule is small enough to do in your head: add the letters' positions (a=1, b=2, …), then take mod 8 — one slot per bucket. cat is 3+1+20 = 24, and 24 mod 8 is 0, so cat lives in bucket 0. Nobody chose bucket 0; the hash picked it. That is the entire contract: store by hash, and retrieval later becomes arithmetic instead of search.

02

Two keys, one bucket

Eight buckets and unbounded keys means collisions are inevitable — two keys will eventually share a slot. owl is 15+23+12 = 50, and 50 mod 8 is 2: dog's bucket. The table doesn't panic; it chains — owl links below dog, and the bucket quietly becomes a list. Chaining is why hash tables degrade gracefully: a crowded bucket is a slightly longer list, not a broken table.

03

Lookup: hash first, compare after

Retrieval runs the same film backwards. Hash the query sun → bucket 6 → one comparison → found. Query owl → bucket 2 → compare against dog: not equal, so follow the chain — found after one compare. Query cow → bucket 1 → empty — not found without touching a single key. Three queries, zero searches: the hash did the walking.

How this page works

The buckets are one LabeledBox component instantiated eight times; the keys are instances with their own fills; every drop-in is a keyframed shift and every formula a timed text swap. The arithmetic is honest — each formula on screen is the actual hash of the key above it.
